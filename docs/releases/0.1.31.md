# LG-XMB 0.1.31

- New wave renderer: a WebGL 2 reconstruction of the PS3's own XMB wave, built
  from the 3.01 runtime capture (the 19x19 spring grid, the FFD programs, the
  bicubic sheet, the Fresnel lookup material and the captured particle records).
  GLSL ES 3.00 only, no WebGL 1 fallback. Without WebGL 2 it draws a static
  backdrop. See `docs/WEBGL2.md`.
- Wave colours gain **PS3 original**: the console's monthly background from the
  recovered `back_colours0` program over the 24 `month_bg` textures, following
  the TV clock or pinned to a month.
- Draws at 60 fps by default, with a **Frame rate** setting for 30. The pacing no
  longer sits on a 33 ms grid, cause the C5's rAF timestamps jitter more than the
  old tolerance allowed and every slip held a frame for three vsyncs.
- Cheaper on the C5's Mali: no preserved drawing buffer, no half-float targets,
  a mediump composite cropped to the rows the sheet can reach, and the monthly
  backdrop cached between frames. Wave FXAA Strong at 60 fps sits around 42% GPU.
- Guard columns at the sheet ends, so the oval hole at the right edge is gone.
- Menu items dim through colour alpha instead of `opacity`, so scrolling the
  menu doesn't hitch the wave any more.
- Particle density is Low / Medium / Normal (500 / 1,000 / 2,000). The capture
  holds 2,025 records, so the old 4,000 choice never drew more than that.
- Menu motion follows the PS3's position curve from the 3.01 firmware: a
  per-frame approach to the target that's 95% there at 200 ms, retargeting from
  wherever the bar or list is. It's one CSS bezier on the compositor, and the
  same key burst on the C5 drops exactly as many frames as the old 160 ms curve.
- Far fewer dropped frames while scrolling. Every key press used to repaint all
  seven visible rows, cause their brightness fell off with distance, and rewrite
  the detail panel in the same frame. Unselected rows and categories now share
  one brightness, like the console, and the detail text follows 140 ms later, so
  a burst repaints it once. On the C5, 100 vertical presses dropped 55 frames
  before and 6 to 14 now.
- Category changes move layers instead of repainting. Every category keeps its
  rows in the page, parked off the right edge and already painted, and each bar
  entry has a dim and a lit face swapped by transform. On the C5 that took a
  category change from dropping a frame about 57 times in 87 presses to about 11,
  for roughly 4 MB of GPU memory and 4 MB in the renderer process. The lit
  category icon no longer grows into place, it's drawn at full size. Note that
  vertical steps measured a little worse with this in (about 23 per 100 against
  about 11), so it's a trade, though the total is well down.
- Particles follow the console's emitter, from the 3.01 particle-motion recovery.
  They're born on the moving wave and launched along its local motion instead of
  being recycled from the capture, so they gather around the sheet. A vertical
  menu step writes a local wind into the 32x16 field and a horizontal press
  raises the jitter for a moment. Menu positions are animated numerically, so
  nothing reads the page's layout per frame.
- Background music is audible again under the home takeover. The TV never
  connects a `com.webos.app.home` stream to the speakers, so the app connects
  its own when playback starts. See `docs/HOME-TAKEOVER.md`.
- The LG Home shortcut under Settings is gone. With the home takeover it only
  relaunched this app, and on a plain install the remote's Home button already
  does the job.
- The old WebGL 1 renderer, its tests and the GitHub workflows are deleted.
  Builds come from `npm run package` on a computer.

Install over the existing app without clearing settings or helper state.
The installed app ID remains `org.local.openxmb.c5` for upgrades.

Known limitations: the wave needs the local reference pack
(`app/ps3-native-data.js` and `app/ps3-background-data.js`), which is
console-derived and gitignored, so a build from the public tree alone shows the
static backdrop. Tested on an LG C5 only. Install and upgrade over 0.1.30 work;
removal and recovery weren't rerun for this build. 1.5x supersampling with Strong
FXAA at 60 fps isn't measured. Other TV/webOS combinations remain untested.

AI-assisted implementation and validation. This remains a development prerelease.
